SLIDE 1 — OPENING
CONNECTING TVET, INDUSTRY & EMPLOYMENT
From Classroom to Career: Bridging the Gap Between Education, Industry and Employment
Puan Salwani NoorCOO, P10X AcademyTVET & Industry Practitioner
Core Message
Education does not end when a student receives a qualification.
The real measure of education is whether that individual is able to enter the real world with:
• Knowledge 
• Competency 
• Confidence 
• Adaptability 
• Professional attitude 
• The ability to create value 
Opening Thought
“The purpose of education is not simply to produce graduates. The purpose is to prepare people to contribute meaningfully to society, industry and the economy.”
SLIDE 2 — THE OPENING QUESTION
WHAT HAPPENS AFTER GRADUATION?
A student spends years in education.
They attend classes.
They complete assignments.
They sit for examinations.
They complete industrial training.
Finally, they graduate.
But then comes the real transition:
From Campus → To Workplace
And suddenly, the questions become different.
Can you perform?
Can you solve problems?
Can you work with people?
Can you communicate with customers?
Can you adapt to new technology?
Can you contribute to the organisation?
The real question:
Are our graduates qualified — or are they truly ready?
SLIDE 3 — QUALIFICATION VS READINESS
**A QUALIFICATION OPENS THE DOOR.
COMPETENCY TAKES YOU FURTHER.**
A qualification is important.
It provides evidence that a person has completed a structured educational journey.
But employment requires something more.
QUALIFICATION
Demonstrates:
• What you have studied 
• What you have learned 
• The level you have achieved 
COMPETENCY
Demonstrates:
• What you can actually perform 
• How you apply knowledge 
• How you solve problems 
• How you respond to real situations 
EMPLOYABILITY
Demonstrates:
• Whether you can contribute 
• Whether you can work professionally 
• Whether you can adapt 
• Whether an organisation can trust you with responsibility 
Key Message
Qualification may get you an interview. Competency, attitude and value determine what happens next.
SLIDE 4 — THE WORLD OF WORK IS CHANGING
THE FUTURE OF WORK IS ALREADY HERE
The world our students are entering is very different from the world we prepared for previously.
Technology is changing:
HOW WE WORK
Automation, AI and digital platforms are changing workplace processes.
WHAT WE WORK WITH
EV, robotics, smart systems, advanced materials and connected technologies are creating new technical requirements.
WHAT CUSTOMERS EXPECT
Customers expect faster, smarter, more personalised and higher-quality services.
HOW BUSINESSES COMPETE
Businesses are increasingly competing through technology, innovation, productivity and talent.
Emerging forces include:
• Artificial Intelligence 
• Automation & Robotics 
• Electric Vehicles 
• Green Technology 
• Digital Transformation 
• Smart Manufacturing 
• Data-driven decision making 
• Industry 4.0 / Industry 5.0 
Key Message
If the industry changes, the skills we develop must change with it.
SLIDE 5 — WHY TVET MATTERS
TVET IS A STRATEGIC DRIVER OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
TVET is sometimes viewed too narrowly as:
“Training people for technical jobs.”
The reality is much bigger.
A strong TVET ecosystem can contribute to:
1. EMPLOYMENT
Providing industries with skilled and competent talent.
2. PRODUCTIVITY
Helping organisations improve quality, efficiency and performance.
3. INNOVATION
Transforming technical knowledge into practical solutions.
4. ENTREPRENEURSHIP
Creating individuals who can build businesses, not only seek employment.
5. SOCIAL MOBILITY
Providing practical pathways for individuals to build sustainable careers.
6. ECONOMIC GROWTH
Supporting industries that require skilled and adaptable human capital.
Key Message
TVET is not a second pathway. It is a strategic pathway for building the workforce and economy of the future.
SLIDE 6 — THE TVET–INDUSTRY GAP
WHERE DOES THE GAP HAPPEN?
There are two environments that students must eventually connect:
THE EDUCATION ENVIRONMENT
Focuses on:
• Curriculum 
• Knowledge 
• Theory 
• Assessment 
• Academic achievement 
• Qualification 
THE INDUSTRY ENVIRONMENT
Focuses on:
• Market demand 
• Performance 
• Productivity 
• Customer expectations 
• Quality 
• Cost 
• Speed 
• Business results 
Neither environment is wrong.
The challenge occurs when:
The transition between the two is too wide.
The question we should ask:
How do we bring industry reality closer to the learning environment?

SLIDE 8 — WHAT INDUSTRY REALLY WANTS
BEYOND THE CERTIFICATE
When employers look at a graduate, they are not only asking:
“What qualification do you have?”
They are also asking:
CAN YOU PERFORM?
Can you execute the technical requirements?
CAN YOU THINK?
Can you identify the root cause of a problem?
CAN YOU COMMUNICATE?
Can you communicate with colleagues, customers and management?
CAN YOU ADAPT?
Can you learn new tools, technology and processes?
CAN YOU WORK WITH OTHERS?
Can you function effectively within a team?
CAN WE TRUST YOU?
Will you take responsibility for your work?
CAN YOU CREATE VALUE?
Will your presence contribute positively to the organisation?
Key Message
Industry hires people for potential — but retains people who create value.
SLIDE 9 — THE EMPLOYABILITY EQUATION
WHAT MAKES A GRADUATE EMPLOYABLE?
KNOWLEDGE
What I understand.
Knowledge gives us the foundation to make informed decisions.
SKILLS
What I can perform.
Skills turn knowledge into action.
EXPERIENCE
What I have practised.
Experience develops confidence, judgement and workplace awareness.
ATTITUDE
How I approach my work.
Technical competency without the right attitude can still create problems.
ADAPTABILITY
How I respond to change.
The future belongs to people who can continuously learn.
Therefore:
KNOWLEDGE + SKILLS + EXPERIENCE + ATTITUDE + ADAPTABILITY = EMPLOYABILITY
But there is another element:
VALUE
The ultimate question is:
“What difference can this person make?”
SLIDE 10 — THE T-SHAPED GRADUATE
THE GRADUATE OF TOMORROW
The future workforce requires both specialisation and adaptability.
THE VERTICAL — DEPTH
Students need deep competency in their chosen discipline.
For example:
• Engineering 
• Automotive 
• IT 
• Manufacturing 
• Mechatronics 
• EV technology 
• Digital technology 
This creates professional expertise.
THE HORIZONTAL — BREADTH
Students also need capabilities that allow them to operate across different environments:
• Communication 
• Leadership 
• Digital literacy 
• Problem-solving 
• Business awareness 
• Collaboration 
• Customer understanding 
• Creativity 
The ideal graduate:
**Deep enough to be an expert.
Broad enough to adapt.**
SLIDE 11 — LEARNING NEVER ENDS
THE END OF STUDY IS NOT THE END OF LEARNING
The traditional career model was:
Study → Graduate → Get a Job → Work
But today's reality is increasingly:
Learn → Apply → Adapt → Relearn → Grow
Technology changes.
Processes change.
Customer expectations change.
Business models change.
Therefore, our skills must also evolve.
This means:
A graduate should not ask only:
“What qualification do I have?”
But also:
“What am I capable of learning next?”
Key Message
Employability is not a destination. It is a continuous capability.
SLIDE 12 — THE ROLE OF UNIVERSITIES
FROM KNOWLEDGE PROVIDER TO TALENT ECOSYSTEM
Universities play a fundamental role in developing:
• Knowledge 
• Research capability 
• Critical thinking 
• Professional discipline 
• Technical understanding 
• Intellectual curiosity 
But industry integration can make learning even more relevant.
Universities can create stronger connections through:
Industry-led projects
Students solve real problems.
Industry mentors
Students learn from practitioners.
Work-based learning
Students experience actual workplace environments.
Industry certification
Students develop recognised technical capabilities.
Industry research
Academic knowledge is connected to real market challenges.
Employment pathways
Students are connected to opportunities before graduation.
Key Message
The question is not whether universities should teach theory or practical skills. We need both — and we need to connect them meaningfully.
SLIDE 13 — THE ROLE OF INDUSTRY
INDUSTRY MUST ALSO COME CLOSER TO EDUCATION
Industry often says:
“Graduates are not industry-ready.”
But industry must also ask:
“How are we participating in preparing them?”
Industry can become more actively involved through:
CO-DESIGN
Help universities understand current industry competency requirements.
CO-TRAIN
Share technology, equipment, processes and practical knowledge.
CO-ASSESS
Help validate whether students can perform according to industry expectations.
CO-INNOVATE
Work with universities on real industry problems.
CO-CREATE
Develop internships, employment pathways and entrepreneurship opportunities.
Key Message
Industry should not only recruit talent. Industry should help develop talent.
SLIDE 14 — THE ROLE OF LECTURERS
LECTURERS AS BRIDGE BUILDERS
A lecturer's influence goes beyond the classroom.
A lecturer can be:
AN EDUCATOR

A FACILITATOR
Creating opportunities for students to learn through doing.
A MENTOR
Helping students understand their strengths and potential.
AN INDUSTRY CONNECTOR
Bringing practitioners, projects and opportunities into the learning environment.
A TALENT DEVELOPER
Preparing students not only to graduate, but to succeed.
A powerful thought:
Students may forget some of the content we teach, but they often remember the people who opened their eyes to what they could become.
